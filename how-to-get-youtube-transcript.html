How to Get a YouTube Transcript Online for Free

A YouTube transcript displays the spoken words of a video as text. It can help you review lessons, take notes, research a topic, and find useful information without replaying the entire video. This guide explains two ways to access available transcript text.

Open Free YouTube Transcript Tool

Method 1: Use YouTube's Transcript Feature

  1. Open the YouTube video you want to review.
  2. Open the video's description or details.
  3. Look for the “Show transcript” option, if available.
  4. Open the transcript and select the text you need, where copying is supported.

The interface can differ between desktop and mobile. Not every video has an accessible transcript, and the option may appear in different places.

Method 2: Get a Transcript with Our Online Tool

  1. Open an individual YouTube video and copy its URL.
  2. Visit our YouTube transcript generator.
  3. Paste the video URL into the input box.
  4. Select “Generate Transcript” and wait for the result.
  5. If text is returned successfully, use “Copy Transcript” or “Download TXT”.

Get YouTube Transcript

How to Copy or Save Transcript Text

After a transcript is generated, use the Copy Transcript button to copy the displayed text. To save it as a file, select Download TXT. You can then open the text file in a compatible text editor. These options are available only when transcript generation succeeds.

Why a YouTube Transcript May Not Be Available

  • No accessible captions: The video may not have transcript text available to the service.
  • Unsupported video: Some video links or video types may not be supported.
  • Temporary service issue: Network problems or service errors can interrupt a request.
  • Request limit: The transcript service may temporarily limit requests.

Try checking YouTube's own “Show transcript” feature or test another video. If the problem continues, wait and try again later.

Are Automatically Generated Transcripts Accurate?

Not always. Speech recognition can misinterpret names, accents, punctuation, numbers, and technical terms. Check important details against the original video before relying on or quoting the text.

What Are Transcripts Useful For?

  • Reviewing lessons and tutorials.
  • Finding key points in interviews and talks.
  • Preparing personal notes and study material.
  • Reading spoken content when listening is inconvenient.
